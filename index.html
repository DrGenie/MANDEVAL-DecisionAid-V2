MandEval – Vaccine Mandate Decision Aid

Use model-based public support, epidemiological benefits and costs to compare vaccine mandate designs across Australia, France and Italy.

Pilot version – based on multi-country DCE evidence

What this tool does

This tool uses stated-preference evidence from a discrete choice experiment (DCE) in Australia, France, and Italy to provide model-based predictions of public support for different vaccine mandate designs. It combines:

  • Mandate design features (scope, exemptions, coverage to lift).
  • Expected lives saved per 100,000 people.
  • Country-specific values of a statistical life (VSL).
  • Implementation cost assumptions.

The tool converts lives saved into monetary benefits and compares them with costs to give summary indicators such as net benefit and benefit–cost ratio (BCR), alongside predicted public support.

How to use the tool

  1. Review the mandate design features used in the DCE under the “Mandate design features” tab.
  2. Set assumptions in the “Settings & assumptions” tab: evaluation period, exposed population and VSL basis.
  3. Configure a mandate in the “Configuration” tab: choose country, outbreak framing, scope, exemptions, coverage and expected lives saved.
  4. Check or adjust costs in the “Costing details” tab.
  5. Click Apply configuration to see model-based public support, benefits, costs, net benefit and BCR in the “Results” tab.
  6. Save scenarios & export summaries, PDFs, Word documents or AI prompts under “Saved scenarios & exports”.

All inputs are editable. Defaults are based on published evidence and can be updated as better data become available.

Interpretation guidance

The tool reports:

  • Total lives saved over the evaluation period.
  • Monetary benefit = total lives saved × value per life saved.
  • Total cost of implementing and enforcing the mandate.
  • Net benefit = monetary benefit − total cost.
  • BCR = monetary benefit ÷ total cost.
  • Model-based public support = predicted probability that the public supports the mandate over the “no mandate” option.

A BCR above 1 indicates that, under the stated assumptions, benefits exceed costs. Public support gives a separate feasibility lens: a mandate with strong net benefits but very low support may be hard to implement.

Costing details – per person and per population

These cost items represent indicative incremental costs of implementing and enforcing vaccine mandates (communication, administration, verification and enforcement), not the full cost of vaccine procurement itself. Values are editable.

Linked to the country selected in the Configuration tab.

Costs are converted into a per-person amount and multiplied by the exposed population and evaluation period set in the Settings & assumptions tab.

Indicative default values

Defaults combine evidence on COVID-19 vaccination programme delivery costs in high-income settings with judgement about the additional effort required for mandates (verification, enforcement and communication). :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}

  • Australia – costs expressed in AUD. Defaults imply mandate-related costs of roughly AUD 20–30 per person over a 3-year period.
  • France – costs expressed in EUR. Defaults imply mandate-related costs of roughly EUR 20–30 per person over a 3-year period.
  • Italy – costs expressed in EUR with similar magnitude, adjustable to match national estimates or implementation plans.

These values should be updated with country-specific costings when available (e.g. detailed budget lines for “health pass”, “Green Pass” or workplace mandates).

Quick reference

Expected lives saved per 100,000
Epidemiological input, treated as a DCE attribute and slider in the tool. Drives both total lives saved and monetary benefit.
Model-based public support
Predicted probability that the public supports the selected mandate over a “no mandate” option, based on the mixed logit model for the chosen country and outbreak scenario.
Lives-saved equivalent (MRS)
For each design feature, the lives-saved equivalent shows how many lives per 100,000 people the average respondent would be willing to trade to obtain a more preferred design (or avoid a less preferred one). In the results tab this is presented in plain language without formulas.
Benefit–cost ratio (BCR)
The ratio of total monetary benefits to total costs. Values above 1 indicate that, under the stated assumptions, the mandate’s benefits exceed its costs.
